AI Business Clone

A clone is a digital replica of a specific business owner — their voice, their expertise, and their judgement — built to draft work in their name. You create one by being interviewed, not by filling in a form, and everything it produces is a draft you review before anything is sent.

A Clone Is Not an Agent

This distinction drives the whole design.

An agent is function-first. The technical SEO agent exists because auditing technical SEO is a job. Its personality is applied so the output reads like something a person wrote. Replace the personality and the agent still does the same job.

A clone is person-first. There is no fixed job. What the clone knows, what it refuses to say, and how it weighs a trade-off are the product. The tasks it performs — a support reply today, a post tomorrow — are interchangeable. The personality is not a presentation layer over a capability; it is the capability.

That is why clones live in their own part of the dashboard rather than inside Agents, and why the interface talks about interviewing a clone and setting its limits, not about configuring its capabilities.

The Five Dimensions

A persona is structured data, not a block of prose, so you can read it field by field, correct it, and see exactly what changes when it evolves.

  • Identity — who the owner is and what the business actually does, in their words rather than marketing copy
  • Voice — formality, directness, warmth, humour, the phrases they genuinely use, and the language they would never use
  • Expertise — domains, methods, credentials, the questions they answer constantly, and where they disagree with their own industry
  • Decision style — what they protect when they cannot have everything, the trade-offs they make routinely, and their risk posture
  • Boundaries — what the clone must never say, never promise, never disclose, and which topics always go to a human

The Interview

The system asks one question at a time, targets whatever it still does not know, and stops when the persona is good enough to use. It asks for examples and actual wording rather than self-description — “what do you say when a client is late paying?” produces far better material than “how would you describe your tone?”, because people describe themselves inaccurately but quote themselves accurately.

Answers are converted into structured fields under strict rules: extract only what was actually said, omit anything not addressed, and never invent detail. Every answer adds to the persona; a later answer can never erase an earlier one.

Drafting

Once a clone knows enough, it can draft replies to customer messages in email, chat, public comment, or internal-note register. Two separate checks protect the owner:

  1. Before drafting — if the incoming message touches a topic the owner marked as theirs to handle, or a confidential subject, the clone does not draft it at all. It is handed straight to the owner with the reason.
  2. After drafting — the generated text is checked in code against the owner’s red lines. A draft that crosses one is still shown, clearly flagged, because hiding it would hide the evidence that something needs fixing.

Messages from customers are treated as untrusted data throughout. They are never mixed into the clone’s instructions, so a crafted message cannot redirect it.

Review and Learning

Every draft ends in one of three verdicts: good as is, edited, or rejected. An edit keeps both what the clone wrote and what the owner actually sent — that difference is the most direct evidence of where the persona is wrong, and it is what drives improvement over time.

The clone never rewrites its own persona silently. Proposed changes are shown as a diff and applied only when the owner accepts them.

Privacy and Isolation

Each clone belongs to one owner, and every read is scoped to them. Operators do not get a cross-client view of clones by default: reading someone’s clone means reading how they think and what they refuse to say, which is a conversation to have with a customer rather than a default setting.

Availability

The Community tier includes one clone — your own. Creating clones for clients, each with a separate persona and isolated data, requires a Business licence.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is an AI business clone different from an AI agent?

An agent is function-first: it exists to perform a job, and its personality is applied so its output reads well. A clone is person-first: what it knows, what it refuses to say, and how it weighs a trade-off are the product itself. The tasks it performs are interchangeable; the person it represents is not.

Does the AI clone send messages on my behalf?

No. The clone is draft-only. It produces text and you review it; you send it yourself. Nothing in the system sends a message to a customer without a human doing it.

How do you build the clone’s persona?

Through an interview, not a form. The system asks adaptive questions across five dimensions - identity, voice, expertise, decision style, and boundaries - and stops when it knows enough to be useful. Answers are extracted into structured fields you can read and correct.

Can I stop the clone from discussing certain topics?

Yes. Boundaries are a first-class part of the persona: phrases it must never say, things it must never promise, topics that always come to you personally, and confidential subjects. Boundaries are enforced twice - written into the prompt and checked against the generated text in code.

Does the clone change over time?

It learns from your edits, but it never rewrites itself silently. When you edit or reject a draft, that is recorded. Proposed changes to the persona are shown to you as a diff and applied only if you approve them.

How many AI clones can I create?

The Community tier includes one clone - your own. Creating clones for clients, each with their own separate persona and isolated data, requires a Business licence.
